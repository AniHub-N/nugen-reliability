Only when the model has the law in front of it. Answering from memory, the aligned model got {{CB_AL_ACC}} of questions right and its confidence_score was noise (AUROC {{CB_AUC}}). What I measured

I aligned llama-v3p2-3b-reasoning on the two documents through the Nugen API, then asked it and its base model 40 questions, each with an answer key checked against the section it comes from: 30 answered somewhere in the Act or the Rules, 10 that sound as if they should be but aren't (Karnataka's fee, GST, stamp duty, a Supreme Court case). Five repeats each, temperature 0.1, once from memory and once with the top four sections from a BM25 search in the prompt. That's 800 calls, scored by deterministic key-fact matching with no LLM judge, and confidence intervals from resampling whole questions.

The score ranks answers but isn't a probability (ECE {{RAG_ECE}}): with the sections, correct answers sit at a median confidence of 90 and wrong or incomplete ones at 81, and above about 88 almost everything is right. From memory the best reading of the stream is the lowest one; with the sections it's the mean. Anyone thresholding this score should check which one they use.

A checker that shows its evidence

About a hundred lines, no model. It refuses any question that names something the documents never mention, and it requires every figure in an answer (days, per cent, rupees, section numbers) to appear in the provisions the model was given. Against Nugen's score on the same responses:

With the sections, the score is the better filter for wrong answers, because most remaining errors are incomplete answers and a figure check can't see a missing fact. The checker's contribution is the unanswerable questions: with it, none got through. Asked for Karnataka's registration fee, both models read Telangana's fee out of the retrieved rule and gave it, the aligned one at confidence 93. The threshold is chosen with each question held out in turn, so these numbers aren't flattered by picking the cut on the answers they're scored on.

What this says about alignment

Nugen's auto-generated benchmark, the 30 Q&A pairs the alignment was built around, shares a fact with nine of my questions. From memory the aligned model got those fully right {{OV_AL}} times out of 45 and the base model {{OV_BA}}. It said the 500 m² registration threshold and the ₹5,000 appeal fee were “not specified” on every repeat. Its answers are also much shorter than the base model's, which costs it with the sections: 39 of its 150 answers there are the first half of a two-part answer. Nugen's own evaluation of the model never ran; the job stayed at CREATED and its status route returned HTTP 500.

The assistant

  1. Scope check. “Karnataka” isn't in the documents, so the question is answered with that fact and no model call.
  2. BM25 over sections and rules; the right one is in the top four for 29 of 30 questions.
  3. The aligned model answers from those four, streamed, with Nugen's confidence alongside.
  4. Figure check against the four provisions, then the confidence threshold. Anything that fails is withheld with the reason and the sections to read instead.

Limits

  • 40 questions, one model, one day. The intervals are wide and say so.
  • Every answer key was checked against its cited section, but the question set hasn't had an independent review.
  • Retrieval and the scope check were tuned lightly on these same questions, so their numbers are in-sample.
  • The figure check can't tell a right number attached to the wrong event. The app's own example: “sixty days from receipt of the appeal” passed it; the Act counts from receipt of the order. The threshold caught that one.
  • Serving was clean: all 800 calls succeeded first try, and the alignment finished in 8 minutes. That's one day's result.
